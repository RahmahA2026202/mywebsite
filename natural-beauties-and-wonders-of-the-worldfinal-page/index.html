5. Angel Falls (Salto Ángel)

Angel Falls, as seen in the popular Disney Pixar movie Up, is the world's tallest uninterrupted waterfall. It is located in Canaima National Park, Gran Sabana region, Bolívar State, Venezuela. It measures 3,212 feet in total height with a 2,648-foot plunge. The source of this waterfall is the Auyán-tepui mountain. It is named after Jimmie Angel, an American aviator who flew over the falls in 1933. It's known in the Pemon language as Kerepakupai Merú (meaning "waterfall of the deepest place"). Want to visit this waterfall? Well, there is a light aircraft and a multi-day river and jungle excursion that you can book.

6. Natural Eternal Flames

A natural eternal flame is a rare geological phenomenon where a continuous underground leak of flammable gases - such as methane, ethane, or propane - escapes through rock fractures and burns indefinitely after being ignited by lightning, human activity, or subterranean heat. While popular consensus often counts roughly 9 major or well-known natural eternal flames globally, geologists note that thousands of smaller or intermittent gas seeps exist worldwide, including ones in: New York, USA; Yanar Dag, Azerbaijan; Baba Gurgur, Iraq; Mount Wingen, Australia; and Murchison, New Zealand. You can hike to visit these.
